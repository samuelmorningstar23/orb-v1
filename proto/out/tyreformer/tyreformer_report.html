Orb TyreFormer

At the end of every lap it forecasts the car's next ten fuel-corrected lap times on its current tyres, with a 90 % band and a cliff risk. It learned from every race and sprint stint of 2023 to 2026, and it is scored on exactly the laps Orb v1's live estimator is scored on.

How it was tested

  • Model selection used only 5-fold cross-validation of 2023–2025, grouped by weekend. That covered seven transformer configurations, the tree ablation, the blend weights and the conformal band widths.
  • 2026 was never used for a choice. The strict test trains on 2023–2025 only. The deployment protocol forecasts race r with a model trained on 2023–2025 plus the 2026 races held before r. One early single-seed probe on 2026 checked the pipeline before any tuning.
  • Sealed holdout. Six weekends are refused by every loader. The model was frozen with , then scored once. Only pooled numbers were written.
  • Baseline. The baseline is the real Orb v1 live estimator. Run with the product's priors, it reproduces its published prefix evaluation exactly. Everywhere else it gets leave-one-out priors.

What is inside

Where it is weaker

  • It uses public timing and telemetry-derived data only. Tyre temperatures, pressures and wear are never observed; the model infers their effect from lap times and energy proxies.
  • The cliff label is dominated by lap-to-lap noise. TyreFormer's probability is calibrated where the estimator's is not, but it is only slightly better than a constant base rate. Show it as a risk, not a call.
  • 2026 is a regulation change. Next-lap bands cover 85–88 % there, against 90 % in cross-validation.
  • The estimator's 2026 priors are leave-one-out within the season, so they draw on later 2026 weekends. That is a small edge in the estimator's favour.
